# Systems Designer — Round 1

## 1. The economy has no numbers, so the pacing targets cannot be checked — BLOCKING
**Problem.** The pacing targets are the whole pitch ("feel clearly stronger on the last one"). Every quantity needed to test them is missing. The GDD gives no gold per room, no gold-to-Essence rate, no boss or elite bonus, no rank cost curve, no unit base stats, no enemy base stats and no upgrade magnitudes. "A first failed run earns enough Essence for 2 to 3 cheap upgrades" and "Rank 1 of every upgrade costs under one average run's Essence" are circular. "Average run" is not defined, and the rank 1 cost is only bounded by the very income that is also unspecified. The targets could be met or missed by any number.
**Source.** "Pacing targets" under Meta progression. "Currency" table (Essence "Converted from gold, plus boss and elite bonuses"). "Upgrade tracks ... cost rises per rank". Enemy scaling is only "proposal: +20% per floor".
**Why it matters.** This is the project's only progression loop. The Risks table treats "Meta pacing too slow" as a risk and answers it with "generous first-run Essence" and "a debug grant", which are still unquantified. The build agents get no values to put in the DataTables.

## 2. The 15-minute session budget does not fit the run length, and the speed tools that make it fit are stretch items — MAJOR
**Problem.** The target is 2 to 3 runs in about 15 minutes, so each run gets roughly 5 minutes. A run that dies "around floor 2" is about 8 to 10 rooms (4 rows plus a boss per floor). Each room is a real-time fight plus a relic pick plus a map pick. That leaves about 30 seconds per room, and no fight duration, time-to-kill or pack size is specified. Success depends on the 4x speed toggle. But the readability goal says a judge "must see why a fight was won or lost", and that pulls against 4x. The 4x toggle and the damage summary are Stretch item 5, below room modifiers and event rooms. The Must-have list includes none of the pacing tools.
**Source.** "Target play session"; "Auto combat" (1x/2x/4x toggle, post-fight summary); the Stretch order; the Risks row "Meta pacing too slow for a 15-minute session".
**Why it matters.** The must-have build can fail the document's own success test.

## 3. Party select is a non-choice in the must-have build, and synergies cannot trigger — MAJOR
**Problem.** The loop says "Party select (pick 4 units)" from a roster of 5. The 5th unit is a meta unlock and Stretch item 1, and the Must-have says "4 party roles". The must-have build therefore offers 4 units for 4 slots: one possible party, and the choice is zero. The synergy lines, which the party select screen is meant to showcase, are always all active. Party composition is one of the player's three stated decisions, and only the relic pick and the pathing remain. The "one big decision" of the loop collapses to two. The final boss is described as punishing "parties with no healer", which cannot happen when the only party has one.
**Source.** "Party and units" (4 slots from 5 units); the Unlocks track; Must have vs. Stretch 1; "Final boss ... punishes parties with no healer".
**Why it matters.** Even with the 5th unit unlocked, the roster is five units and four slots, so the only choice is which unit to bench. The "real choice" claim is thin.

## 4. In-run difficulty and state carry-over are undefined, so the power curve cannot be reasoned about — MAJOR
**Problem.** Meta upgrades are flat, permanent and global (+HP, +Attack, +Defense). Relics are in-run and mostly "+10% party Attack". Unit level-ups add "flat stats" and reset each run. Against these, enemies scale +20% per floor. No document section shows whether the player's power outruns enemy power across floors, so it is unknown whether "dies around floor 2" is realistic. HP persistence between rooms is never stated. The Rest room "heals 30%" and a modifier "party starts at 80% HP" both imply HP carries over. The GDD does not say whether cooldowns, shields or buffs carry over either. The map choice also carries almost no information. Elite is "better loot" with no quantified reward, and there is no stated cost of a lost elite fight beyond the wipe. The only run-ending condition is a wipe, so risk and reward on the map are unbalanced by default. Nothing explains why a player would pick treasure or rest over elite.
**Source.** Room type table; "Unit XP ... flat stats"; "Scaling ... +20% per floor"; the room modifiers.
**Why it matters.** The difficulty curve is the only thing connecting the two Director's Cut features, and it is not designed.

## 5. Gold is a dead currency and economy upgrades stack redundantly — MINOR
**Problem.** Gold has no in-run use ("Nothing in the jam scope") and exists only to become Essence. It is a second counter on one pipeline. The design then offers the "+gold per room" upgrade, the "+Essence per run" upgrade and the "double gold" room modifier. These three multiply the same single stream, and the Essence-per-run upgrade makes the other two invisible. Rank 1 prices are also "under one average run's Essence", which tells the player nothing about the top ranks. The Run start track ("Begin with a common relic") duplicates what the Common relic does in a run. The GDD itself lists whether gold has an in-run use as an open question, so the decision is acknowledged but not made.
**Source.** "Two currencies" table; "Economy" track; "Room modifiers"; open question on gold.
**Why it matters.** Systems that coexist without interlocking. Giving gold a role, or deleting it, would simplify the economy.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

### Conflicts

**Player Psychologist F4 and Business Analyst F1 vs. my F2 (room count and time per room).** The Psychologist budgets about 15 rooms per run and about 20 seconds per room. I budgeted 8 to 10 rooms per run, about 30 seconds, because the GDD's own pacing target says the first run "dies around floor 2". Both figures come from the same missing quantity, which is fight length. My side: the two numbers describe different runs, and the budget has to be computed per run number. Run 1 is short (about 8 to 10 rooms). By the pitch, run 3 should go deeper, so it is the longest run, and it must fit in whatever time is left. The 15-minute target is therefore harder than either figure suggests, because the run that matters most for the "feel stronger" payoff is the one with the least time. A flat per-room average hides this.

**Business Analyst F3 vs. my F4 and F5 (what is "decorative").** The Business Analyst lists seeded RNG and the debug panel as decorative spend. I disagree in part. The debug grant is the only stated mitigation for my F1 (economy pacing). Without some way to set Essence or inject runs, nobody can tune an economy that has no numbers. It is cheap, and it should stay. I agree that the 4-direction facing work is the real cut candidate.

**Feasibility Lead F5 and Adversarial QA F2 (seed determinism) vs. my F1.** They are right that one shared stream breaks "same seed, same floors". The tension with my lens is that economy tuning is the one place where reproducibility would matter most, since pacing can only be tested by replaying comparable runs. If combat rolls and map rolls share a stream, I cannot compare two economy tunings on the same map. I rate this higher than their MINOR in the Feasibility Lead's review, but only as it feeds balance work.

### Connections

**Adversarial QA F1 (no fight end condition) combined with my F2 and F4.** I did not catch the stalemate. It is also a pacing problem, and it is a bigger one than the room-count arithmetic. A heal-and-shield mirror (Shaman, Warden, "enemies shielded") means fight duration has no upper bound, so my 30 seconds per room is not just unspecified but unbounded. Combined with my F4, there is no stated relationship between party damage output and enemy sustain, and the +20% per floor scaling in my F4 could push enemy healing past the player's damage at exactly the floors where runs end. A single enrage or round cap in the GDD would fix the softlock and give the session budget a worst-case number. I would make this an input to my F2, not a separate issue.

**Feasibility Lead F2 ("4 room types" vs. weight tables for 5) combined with my F4.** My F4 says Elite has no quantified reward and no stated tradeoff against Treasure or Rest. Feasibility notes the must-have lists only 4 room types while the weight tables tune 5. If Elite or Rest is the room cut, then my risk-and-reward problem becomes a map with two or three types and almost no choice at all. Adversarial QA's note that Event weights of 10 to 15% leave the table at 85 to 90% without events shows the room-type decision has to be made before any economy numbers can be written.

**Adversarial QA F4 (mid-run exit) combined with my F1 and F5.** Gold converts to Essence only on the results screen. Adding the "+Essence per run" upgrade means the multiplier applies at one point, so an abandoned run loses both gold and the bonus. This makes the one-sink economy brittle, and it is another reason to give gold an in-run use or drop it. That would also remove the need for the conversion event.

### Revisions

- **F5: upgrade from MINOR to MAJOR.** Adversarial QA pointed out that "+Essence per run" and "+gold per room" compound with no stated cap or cost curve. I had called them redundant. They are worse than that. With no curve, stacking can make the income side of my F1 unbounded, so this is an economy-breaking risk and not a cleanliness issue.
- **F3: keep MAJOR, drop one claim.** The Psychologist, Feasibility Lead and Adversarial QA all reached the same conclusion, so I am not adding to it. I would soften my last sentence in "Why it matters". Even with five units, benching one is a real choice, if the synergies differ by pairing. The GDD should show that they do. The finding is mainly about the must-have build.
- **F2: add the dependency on Adversarial QA F1.** My budget has to carry a worst case, and the document does not give one. I leave the rating at MAJOR, but I now see it as blocked until a fight-end rule exists.
- **F1: unchanged.** No colleague's finding gives any of the missing numbers, and three reviewers (Psychologist F5, Adversarial QA F5, Business Analyst F1) depend on this gap without being able to close it.
